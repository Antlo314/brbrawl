BATTLE RAP BRAWL · ART AND AUDIO

Seedream 32-bit prompt pack

Every piece of art for the game, in late-90s arcade fighting-game style. The battlers are drawn as sprite sheets: 9 poses in one image, so each character is about 9 generations instead of 40. Tap a prompt to copy it, attach what it says, and save the result with the file name shown.

How it works

  1. Get reference photos of each battler (see the References tab). Full-body photos in their fight-night outfit work best.
  2. Make the master sprite (master.jpg) for each battler from those photos. Get it perfect and approved. It goes in the game right away as their standing pose.
  3. Make the 4 sprite sheets, attaching master.jpg each time. Each sheet holds 9 poses, and the game cuts them apart automatically.
  4. Make the extras: effects sheet, portrait, nameplate and super title. Then the stage and the text.
  5. Drop the downloads into the MM Game folder on your Desktop and ask Claude to sort them. It reads each image, names it, and makes a game-sized copy. Or save them yourself as JPG with the exact name shown.

Why sheets work better: poses drawn together in one image come out with the same face, outfit and scale. That's much more consistent than 35 separate generations, and far fewer prompts.

When one pose on a sheet comes out wrong:

  • Use Seedream's area edit on just that cell, or
  • use "Fix one pose" at the bottom of the character tab. It makes that single pose as its own image (e.g. kick_2.jpg), which replaces that cell in the game.

Rules that keep it clean

  • No green in the art. The game deletes the green background, and anything green in an outfit or effect disappears with it. Mook's ice stays pale blue, never teal.
  • Use the aspect ratio listed on each prompt. Sprite sheets are 1:1.
  • Poses must not touch. If two poses on a sheet overlap or bleed into the next cell, re-roll. The game finds the gaps between poses, but it can't separate two that are touching.
  • Check spelling on every text image.
  • Keep game and company names out of the prompts. The style description gets the look and keeps the characters original.
  • Chunkier pixels: set pixel: 3 in fighters.js, or 0 for no pixel snapping.

Reference photos

There are no free-to-use photos of either battler online. Wikimedia Commons has nothing for them. Below is what exists publicly. Use it to study each battler's look; for the images you actually upload to Seedream, photos from the battlers themselves are the clean option.

Best option: ask each battler's team for 4 phone photos in the outfit they want in the game, against a plain wall:

  1. Full body, straight on
  2. Full body, side view facing right
  3. Face close-up
  4. Their fight stance or signature battle pose

Asking also gets you their OK to use their likeness, which you need anyway.
